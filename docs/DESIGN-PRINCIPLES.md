## 1. How to use this document

These fifteen principles are **binding constraints, not aspirations.** Code that violates one is a
defect: it must be fixed, or it must carry a recorded waiver (§3).

They are derived from the project's own documents — the [charter](planning/01-project-charter.md),
[architecture](planning/03-solution-architecture.md),
[conceptual design](planning/04-conceptual-design.md) and
[decision log](planning/00-decision-log.md) — not from generic best practice. Each one exists because
something specific in this project would otherwise go wrong, and each names what that is.

**Relationship to other instruction files.** The invariants in `CLAUDE.md` are the short, always-loaded
subset an agent must never violate even without reading anything else. This document is the full,
reasoned set. Where they overlap they agree; where `CLAUDE.md` is terser, this is authoritative.

**When principles conflict with a deadline, the principles hold and scope is cut instead**
([DL-10](planning/00-decision-log.md#dl-10--build-a-steel-thread-to-gw1-rather-than-deferring-the-build),
[E0 §5](planning/epics/E0-steel-thread-gw1.md#5-emergency-cut)). The structural properties these
protect are cheap to build in and expensive to retrofit; features are neither. A deadline is a reason
to ship less, never a reason to ship it badly wired.

## 2. The principles

### Structure — how the system is divided

---

#### DP-01 — Sources are plugins; the core never names one

**Only the source layer may know that a particular data provider exists.** No module outside
`pipeline/src/fpl_dof/sources/` may import, name, or branch on a specific source. Everything
downstream consumes the conformed model.

**Why here.** [DL-05](planning/00-decision-log.md#dl-05--data-sources-fpl-api-understatfbref-bookmaker-odds--behind-a-pluggable-adapter-layer)
records the owner's explicit requirement for easy onboarding of future sources. Three sources ship
initially and more are anticipated (OD-04). A source name leaking into a model turns "add an adapter"
into "audit the whole pipeline".

**In practice**
- Adding a source touches exactly one new adapter module, one config entry, and its tests — nothing else.
- Cross-cutting behaviour (rate limiting, retry, caching, snapshotting, user-agent, credit budgeting)
  lives in the adapter base class and is never reimplemented per source.
- Where two sources supply the same field, precedence is **configuration, not code**.
- `fetch` never parses; `parse` never conforms; `to_canonical` never applies business logic.

**Violated when** a model imports `understat`; a transform branches on `if source == "fbref"`;
an adapter computes a derived metric; precedence is expressed as an `if` chain.

**Verified by** import-lint in CI (the H2 hook and its CI counterpart), and by the acceptance test in
[Design §14.1](planning/04-conceptual-design.md#141-adding-a-data-source): if onboarding a source
touched anything else, the abstraction has leaked and that is the defect.

---

#### DP-02 — Prediction and decision are separate concerns

**The model answers "what is this player worth?" The optimiser answers "given those values, what
should I do?" They are separate packages communicating through a typed contract.** The optimiser
never imports a model; the model never knows a squad exists.

**Why here.** [Blueprint B5](planning/02-project-plan-and-blueprint.md). It is what lets the
deterministic MILP be replaced by a stochastic layer without touching the solver
([DL-06](planning/00-decision-log.md#dl-06--decision-engine-is-a-multi-gameweek-milp-with-explicit-chip-planning)),
and what lets [E4-S4a](planning/epics/E4-decision-engine.md)'s simulation re-rank sit between them as
a new stage rather than a rewrite.

**In practice**
- The contract carries **mean and variance** from day one, even while the solver only approximates
  its use of variance (CLAUDE.md Invariant 6).
- A change to how points are forecast requires no optimiser change, and vice versa.
- Both sides are independently testable with fixtures, and are.

**Violated when** the optimiser recomputes an expected value; a model reasons about budget or squad
legality; the two are tuned together against a single end-to-end metric.

---

#### DP-03 — Pure core, effectful edge

**Rules, constraint construction, scoring arithmetic, price mechanics and transfer accounting are
pure functions.** No I/O, no clock reads, no global state, no config loading inside them —
configuration arrives as an argument.

**Why here.** These are the modules where a bug is silent and total: an off-by-one in selling-price
arithmetic misprices every transfer for a season. Purity is what makes the 100% coverage requirement
in NFR-08 achievable rather than aspirational, and what makes property-based testing possible at all.

**In practice**
- I/O happens at the boundary: adapters read, publishers write, everything between is a
  transformation.
- Anything time-dependent takes the time as a parameter. This is also what makes deadline logic
  testable across the BST/AEDT transition ([DL-11](planning/00-decision-log.md#dl-11--store-and-compute-in-utc-render-in-local-time-only-at-the-ui-edge)).
- Randomness takes an explicit seed.

**Violated when** a rules function calls `datetime.now()`, reads an environment variable, or hits the
network; a test needs a fixture directory to check an arithmetic result.

---

#### DP-04 — Every seam is a versioned, typed contract

**Where two components meet — especially across a language or process boundary — the interface is
explicit, versioned, and generated from a single definition rather than written twice.**

**Why here.** [DL-04](planning/00-decision-log.md#dl-04--python-for-data-and-analytics-typescriptreact-for-the-web-app)
creates a two-language monorepo, and
[DL-14](planning/00-decision-log.md#dl-14--the-web-data-contract-carries-the-rules-configuration)
shows the failure mode concretely: without a shared definition, FPL's squad rules get implemented
twice and drift.

**In practice**
- `contracts/` holds the single schema definition; Python validators and TypeScript types are both
  **generated** from it. Neither is hand-written.
- The published web contract is versioned (`/data/v1/...`). Breaking changes bump the version and
  publish both during transition, so a stale cached client never breaks.
- Cross-language conformance tests fail the build when two implementations disagree (DL-14).
- The model→optimiser contract (DP-02) is a typed interface, not a shared dataframe convention.

**Violated when** a TypeScript type is hand-maintained alongside a Python schema; a field is added to
a published artefact without a version consideration; two components agree by convention rather than
by contract.

### Adaptability — how it changes safely

---

#### DP-05 — Business rules are configuration, in every language

**No FPL rule value appears as a literal in code, in any language.** Scoring values, squad
composition, budget, club limits, formation legality, transfer costs, rollover caps, price and
selling-value mechanics, chip definitions and expiries all live in configuration, seeded from the
API's own game settings where exposed.

**Why here.** CLAUDE.md Invariants 2 and 9. FPL changes rules most seasons — Defensive Contribution
arrived in 2025/26 and the BPS matrix was revised for 2026/27. A rule change must be a configuration
edit plus a conformance test, not an archaeology exercise. **A literal `4` for a forward goal is a bug
even when the number is right**, because the next season it will be wrong and nobody will know where
to look.

**In practice**
- The `fpl-rules` skill is the reference for *what the values should be*; config is where they live.
- Values carry a verification date and a source, and are labelled where they are inferred rather than
  officially published — the full BPS matrix is community-reverse-engineered, not confirmed.
- The rules configuration is published as `rules.json` in the web contract so the browser validator
  is parameterised from the same source (DL-14).
- Historical data from a different scoring regime is handled explicitly, never assumed comparable.

**Violated when** a `3` for the club limit appears in a `.tsx` file; a scoring value is duplicated
between config and a docstring that then drifts; a test asserts a hardcoded expected points total
that would silently pass under changed rules.

---

#### DP-06 — Every tunable is named, defaulted and justified

**A number that could reasonably be different is a parameter, not a literal — and every parameter
carries a name, a default, a valid range, what it trades off, and how it was last set.**

**Why here.** This project is full of quantities that are explicitly to be tuned later and are
recorded as open questions: the planning horizon (Q-01), the discount factor γ (Q-02), the bench
weight β (Q-03), the club cap in the starting XI (Q-12), shrinkage strength, the risk-dial λ, the
candidate-pool size. These are the knobs the season is spent turning. A magic number buried in a
function is a knob nobody can find.

**In practice**
- Parameters are declared in configuration with documentation alongside, not scattered as defaults in
  function signatures.
- Each records **why the current value**: measured, assumed, or inherited — and if measured, from what.
- "Tune this later" is a parameter plus an open question ID, never a `# TODO` on a literal.
- Changing a parameter must not require a code change.

**Violated when** `0.85` appears inline in the objective function; a shrinkage constant is chosen in a
notebook and copied into code with no record of why; two modules independently define what should be
one parameter.

---

#### DP-07 — Build vertical slices that each exit usable

**Every increment is a thin path through all the layers it needs, ending in something that works.
Never a horizontal layer waiting on another layer to become useful.**

**Why here.** [Blueprint B2](planning/02-project-plan-and-blueprint.md), and the entire structure of
[E0](planning/epics/E0-steel-thread-gw1.md). This is the primary defence against R-05 — a solo
project stalling mid-build and leaving a half-connected system that is worse than none. It is also
what makes the epic sequence reprioritisable: a plan of usable slices can be re-ordered, a plan of
layers cannot.

**In practice**
- The steel thread runs end-to-end through every layer before any layer is thickened.
- An epic that cannot be stopped after any of its stories and still leave a working system is
  mis-decomposed.
- Thin does not mean shortcut: the structural properties in DP-01 to DP-05 are present in the
  thinnest slice, because they are cheap to include and expensive to retrofit.

**Violated when** a story delivers "the data layer" with no consumer; a refactor lands that nothing
uses yet; work is sequenced so nothing is demonstrable until three stories have all landed.

---

#### DP-08 — New behaviour ships dark, and is promoted on evidence

**Model and decision changes land behind a flag, run in shadow mode, and are promoted only when
evidence says they are better — never because they are finished.**

**Why here.** [E8 §5](planning/epics/E8-in-season-operations.md) sets the bar precisely, and R-17
names the failure: retraining monthly on ten gameweeks of the noisiest target in the project is how a
working system quietly gets worse. Mid-season is when every model change will actually be proposed,
and it is the worst possible time to change something on intuition.

**In practice**
- Shadow mode publishes a candidate's predictions for comparison without letting them influence
  anything.
- Promotion requires all three E8 conditions: backtest regression improves on the **held-out** season;
  live rolling accuracy does not degrade over **at least six** shadow gameweeks; and the change is
  **explicable in advance**. A change that only improves the metric found a pattern in this season's noise.
- **Prefer shrinkage to refitting** — blending toward current-season evidence in proportion to how
  much evidence exists degrades gracefully; a fresh fit on ten gameweeks lurches.
- **One exception:** a *bug fix* is not a model change and does not wait. If it is being argued about,
  it is a model change.

**Violated when** a model is retrained and deployed in one step; a flag is added and immediately
defaulted on; a change is promoted after two good gameweeks.

### Transparency — how it stays understandable

---

#### DP-09 — Every number carries its derivation, its uncertainty and its provenance

**A figure the system shows or acts on must be traceable to its inputs, must state how confident it
is, and must say where it came from when that is not obvious.**

**Why here.** [Blueprint B6](planning/02-project-plan-and-blueprint.md) — the system exists to make
the owner a better manager, not to replace them, and a recommendation that cannot be explained will
not be trusted or followed. The uncertainty half is CLAUDE.md Invariant 6 and the E0 review gate: the
GW1 forecast is knowingly unvalidated, and presenting it as a measurement would be actively harmful.
The provenance half comes from OD-06 — effective ownership is **not directly observable** (CON-12),
so however it is obtained, the UI must say which route is in use. *A risk dial driven by an estimated
quantity presented as a measured one is worse than no risk dial.*

**In practice**
- Expected points decompose by scoring component, and the decomposition is a product feature, not a
  debugging aid.
- Every forecast displays an uncertainty range. **A predicted 5.2 points that could be anything from
  0 to 15 must never render like a measurement.**
- Estimated, sampled and measured quantities are visually distinguishable, and named as such.
- Every recommendation shows what it beat and by how much, and always presents "do nothing" as a
  first-class option.

**Violated when** a bare number appears with no interval; an estimate is displayed identically to an
observation; a recommendation is shown without its runner-up.

---

#### DP-10 — Prefer the formulation you can argue with

**Where two designs are close in expected quality, choose the one whose output a human can
interrogate and challenge.** Explainability is a design driver, not a feature added afterwards.

**Why here.** [DL-15](planning/00-decision-log.md#dl-15--chip-timing-by-scenario-enumeration-highs-as-the-solver-from-e4)
is this principle applied: chip timing is decided by **enumerating scenarios** rather than by MILP
decision variables, and one of the three stated reasons is that *"Free Hit in GW18 beats GW17 by 4.1
points and beats not playing it by 9.3"* is a sentence you can argue with, whereas a chip binary
flipping inside a solver is not. Chip timing is the recommendation most likely to be challenged, and
an unarguable recommendation is one that gets overridden on instinct or followed on faith — both bad.

**In practice**
- Prefer enumeration over opaque search where the space is small enough.
- Prefer a chain of small models with observable intermediate outputs over one monolith, unless the
  monolith is *materially* better — and if it is, that trade-off is a recorded decision (Q-04).
- Keep runners-up and margins, not just winners.
- If a design cannot explain its own output, that is a cost to weigh, not a detail to defer.

**Violated when** an approach is chosen purely on benchmark score with no account of interpretability;
intermediate results are discarded; the answer to "why did it pick that?" is "the solver decided".

### Trust — how you know it is right

---

#### DP-11 — Every run is reproducible from its recorded inputs

**For any output the system ever produced, it must be possible to reconstruct exactly what it knew at
the time and why it advised what it did.**

**Why here.** NFR-06 as amended by
[DL-13](planning/00-decision-log.md#dl-13--charter-amendments-following-the-2026-08-09-architecture-and-plan-audit),
and it is what makes the weekly retrospective in E8 honest rather than a rationalisation. Without it,
"the model was right and I overrode it" is an unfalsifiable memory.

**In practice**
- Immutable bronze snapshots, a `run_id` threaded through every stage, pinned dependencies, and the
  effective resolved configuration recorded in the manifest.
- **Logical, not byte-for-byte, reproducibility** — DL-13 downgraded this deliberately: a MILP over a
  degenerate problem has no deterministic optimum and threaded gradient boosting is not bitwise
  stable. The standard is: byte-identical silver, identical objective within tolerance, identical
  published decisions, backed by a **deterministic tie-break** in the optimiser.
- Determinism where it is affordable — explicit seeds, stable sort orders, fixed tie-breaks — because
  the cheapest reproducibility is the kind you never had to reconstruct.

**Violated when** an output cannot be tied to the inputs that produced it; a run depends on ambient
state; two identical inputs produce different published decisions with no tie-break to explain it.

---

#### DP-12 — Measure skill against a baseline, never in absolute terms

**Every quality metric is expressed as improvement over a stated, dumb baseline. A threshold a
no-edge model could pass is not a threshold.**

**Why here.** [DL-13 amendment 1](planning/00-decision-log.md#dl-13--charter-amendments-following-the-2026-08-09-architecture-and-plan-audit)
caught exactly this: the original `MAE ≤ 2.1` sat in the range a *constant predictor* achieves, and
`Spearman ≥ 0.30` across all positions is plausibly reachable knowing only price and position. Both
would have certified a model with no edge whatsoever. The charter's tier-2 metrics are now
skill-over-baseline, with top-20 precision added as the decision-relevant measure.

**In practice**
- Every metric names its baseline — typically a `(position, price)` predictor, the template team, or
  the overall average.
- Backtests report against the naive strategy, not only against themselves.
- Sample size is respected: the captaincy hit rate over one season is n=38, so the standard error is
  around 8 points and 45% versus 55% is indistinguishable. **Metrics too noisy to act on in-season are
  backtest gates only**, and must say so.
- **"It improved" is not a finding. "It improved relative to X, by more than the noise in X" is.**

**Violated when** a metric is quoted with no comparator; a model change is accepted on an absolute
score; an in-season metric movement within its own error bars triggers action.

---

#### DP-13 — Test hardest where being wrong is invisible

**Testing effort follows the probability that a defect is silent, not code volume or uniform coverage
targets.**

**Why here.** [Design §10](planning/04-conceptual-design.md#10-testing). This project's dangerous bugs
do not throw: a mismatched player attributes one footballer's xG to another (R-10); a leaked feature
makes the model look better (R-04); an off-by-one in free-transfer accrual builds a plan on transfers
that do not exist. Nothing goes red. The output just becomes quietly wrong.

**In practice**
- **Property-based tests** for optimiser legality: for *any* generated input, the returned squad
  satisfies every FPL rule — at *every* gameweek of the horizon, not just the first.
- **Conformance tests** as ground truth: recompute historical gameweek points from raw stats and
  reconcile against FPL's published totals. This simultaneously validates the rules engine, the data
  layer and entity resolution against reality.
- **Contract tests** against recorded responses, so upstream schema drift surfaces in CI and not at a
  deadline.
- **The safety mechanisms are themselves tested** — inject bad data and prove the quality gate blocks.
  An untested gate is a gate you find out about on the day it matters.
- Fault injection per source, proving degradation (DP-15).
- 100% coverage on rules and constraint modules specifically; elsewhere, coverage follows risk.

**Violated when** a quality gate has never been shown to block; a constraint has an example test but
no property test; effort goes to raising a coverage percentage rather than to the modules where
wrongness is silent.

---

#### DP-14 — Every artefact traces to a requirement

**Code, tests, gates and decisions all name what they exist for.** The chain runs
requirement → epic story → commit → test → gate, and it must be walkable in both directions.

**Why here.** The charter numbers every requirement (FR-01…, NFR-01…) precisely so this is possible,
and it is what lets scope be cut under deadline pressure without cutting something load-bearing —
you can see what a deletion would orphan. It is also how a future session, or a different agent,
discovers *why* something exists before removing it.

**In practice**
- Commits name the requirement IDs they implement.
- Epic stories name the requirements they serve; every requirement appears in some story or is
  explicitly deferred.
- Quality gates name the requirement they protect.
- Tests for a requirement are discoverable from it.
- Decisions go in the decision log **before** implementation, and are superseded rather than edited.

**Violated when** a change lands with no requirement reference; a requirement exists that no story
covers and no decision defers; a gate fails and nobody can say what it was protecting.

---

#### DP-15 — Degrade, never break

**Loss of any non-essential input reduces quality; it never stops the system.**

**Why here.** NFR-15. Three of four data sources are scraped or credit-capped and *will* fail —
Understat and FBref break on layout changes (R-06), odds credits run out (R-08). The system must
still produce a recommendation before the deadline, because a slightly worse recommendation
comfortably beats no recommendation at 03:30 local time.

**In practice**
- Each source declares which fields it contributes; losing it removes those and nothing else.
- Degraded state is **visible, not silent** — the manifest records it and the UI shows it. Silent
  degradation is worse than failure, because the output looks normal.
- The optimiser has a greedy fallback when the solver does not converge; the result is flagged as
  fallback-quality. R-07 is rated High, so this is not optional.
- **The one thing that must not degrade quietly is correctness**: a failing quality gate blocks
  publication and the last good artefact stays live (CLAUDE.md Invariant 7). Stale and honest beats
  fresh and wrong.

**Violated when** a scraper failure aborts the pipeline; a missing source silently substitutes a
default that looks like real data; a fallback result is presented indistinguishably from a full solve.

## 3. Waivers

A principle may be knowingly broken. It may not be *quietly* broken.

### Procedure

1. **Add an inline waiver marker** at the code site, in a comment:

   ```
   DP-WAIVER(DP-06): candidate pool size fixed at 220 pending Q-01 measurement — see DL-nn
   ```

   Format: `DP-WAIVER(<principle-id>): <reason> — see <decision-id>`

2. **Record a decision-log entry** in [`docs/planning/00-decision-log.md`](planning/00-decision-log.md)
   stating which principle, why, what it costs, and what would let it be removed.

3. **Reference the decision ID in the marker.** A waiver without one is not a waiver, it is a
   violation with a comment attached.

### Rules

- Waivers are **greppable by design**: `rg "DP-WAIVER"` returns every live one. Review them at each
  epic boundary.
- A waiver is a debt, not an amendment. It records that the principle still applies and this code does
  not meet it.
- **Deadline pressure is not a waiver reason.** DL-10 settled that: principles hold and scope gets cut
  instead. A waiver justified only by "no time" is a scope decision in disguise, and should be taken
  as one.
- If the same waiver recurs three times, the principle is probably wrong or badly stated — propose an
  amendment rather than accumulating waivers.

## 4. Amendment procedure

**No agent may amend this document.** A `PreToolUse` hook denies writes to it.

To amend:

1. **The owner must explicitly approve the change in conversation**, having seen the specific proposed
   wording.
2. Record a decision-log entry describing the amendment and its rationale.
3. The owner edits the file, or explicitly authorises an agent to do so in that conversation.
4. Bump the version and the `Adopted` date in the header.
5. Amend the summary table in [§1](#the-fifteen-in-brief) and the pointer at
   `.claude/rules/design-principles.md` in the same change.

**Principles may be added, clarified or retired. They may not be silently weakened.** Retiring one
requires stating what now prevents the failure it existed to prevent.

### Review points

| When | Review |
| --- | --- |
| End of each epic | Are the live waivers still justified? Did any principle obstruct without benefit? |
| After GW1 | Did the principles survive real deadline pressure, or were they quietly abandoned? |
| End of season | Full review. Which earned their keep, which were ceremony? |
